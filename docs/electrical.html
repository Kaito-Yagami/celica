Before you add another one

What a fuse tap actually does

The fuse is not the limit — the wire is

An add-a-circuit does not create a new supply. It hangs your load on the same feed wire and the same junction block trace that already serves the original circuit. You can fit a 15 A fuse in the tap leg and still be over the limit of the 0.85 mm² wire feeding the position. Size the tap against the original circuit's wiring, not against the plastic you plugged into it.

Budget to 80 %, not 100 %

A blade fuse is designed to carry its rating indefinitely and blow well above it — which means it will happily sit at 95 % while the wire behind it warms up for years. Every figure on this page uses 80 % of rating as the usable ceiling.

Anything over about 5 A wants a relay

Heated seats are the example on this car: roughly 8 A for the pair. That belongs on its own fused feed from the battery, switched by a relay whose coil is the only thing touching an existing circuit. A coil draws about 150 mA — that is what a fuse tap is for.

Verify, don't assume

Class probe

Because the manual's fusebox is the wrong one, every circuit class here is inferred, not sourced. Put a test lamp or a meter on the slot, work through the four key positions, and this will tell you what you actually have — and remember it.
